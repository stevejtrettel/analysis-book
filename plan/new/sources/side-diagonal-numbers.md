# Side-and-Diagonal Numbers — Sources

Sourcing note for the oscillating ancient √2 process (load-bearing in
Ch 1 stories, Ch 2's "sup is not the general endpoint" moment, Ch 3's
motivation of the limit definition, Ch 4's splitting section).
Web-checked 2026-08-18; ⚠ VERIFY items need a library pass.

## The mathematics (checkable directly)

Recurrence: s₁ = d₁ = 1; sₙ₊₁ = sₙ + dₙ, dₙ₊₁ = 2sₙ + dₙ. The ratios
dₙ/sₙ = 1/1, 3/2, 7/5, 17/12, 41/29, … satisfy dₙ² − 2sₙ² = ±1
(alternating sign — the Pell identity, same one behind the Babylonian
thread's April exercise), are exactly the continued-fraction
convergents of √2, and alternate around √2: 1 < 7/5 < 41/29 < … <
√2 < … < 17/12 < 3/2. Hence sup of the sequence = 3/2 and inf = 1,
while the endpoint is √2 — strictly inside. Sup is the wrong notion
of endpoint for this process.

## The history

- Theon of Smyrna, *On Mathematics Useful for the Understanding of
  Plato* (~AD 130), records the side-and-diagonal recurrence and the
  ±1 identity. ⚠ VERIFY exact passage (Hiller edition / Lawlor
  translation) before citing in print.
- Proclus, *Commentary on the Republic*, displays the pairs (1,1),
  (2,3), (5,7), (12,17) and connects them to Plato's Republic 546c
  ("rational diameter of five" = 7/5 — the SAME passage as the
  ἄρρητος attestation in `unspeakable.md`; the two source notes
  meet here). Attribution to the Pythagoreans is via Proclus and is
  scholarly-standard but inferential — say "recorded by Theon,
  likely Pythagorean," not "the Pythagoreans wrote."
- The names: "side numbers" and "diameter/diagonal numbers" — the
  ratio tends to diagonal/side of the isosceles right triangle.
