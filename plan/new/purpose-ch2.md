## Story

- Seed (decided): the axiomatic method as plot — saying what numbers
  *should be* brings them into existence. Section rule: every section
  is a definition section — an item from Ch 1's list of nameless
  things gets its formal definition. Twist ending:
  uncountability. Required pivot: the doctrine reframes from "each
  number needs a name" to "the *system* needs a definition."
- **Opening move (decided in the story discussion): promotion.** Ch 1
  closed on the unified diagnosis (every gap = a bounded set with no
  least upper bound); this chapter opens by promoting the pattern the
  practice kept reaching for to the defining axiom. Historical roots
  available at the promotion moment: Eudoxus (proportion by rational
  bounds — Dedekind's acknowledged ancestor), Bolzano 1817 (the lub
  principle debuts in his attempt to prove IVT — ties to Ch 1's
  calculus forward-glance), Dedekind 1858/1872 (driven to the cuts by
  teaching calculus).
- **Geometry section (allocation decided — see
  `axiomatic-measurement.md` header):** the definitions denote ("the
  circle has a circumference" as a theorem), trapping in sup form,
  the forcing theorem, confirmation obligations doubling as the
  sup-manipulation practice the chapter needs anyway, trig at
  constructible angles. Closes NO
  sandwiches (parabola → Ch 3, circle → Ch 4): this chapter proves
  the numbers *exist*; later chapters prove the traps *close*.
- **Section order (decided 2026-08-18, Steve; decimals removed later
  the same day): the rising shape, five sections.** Axiom and sup
  tools (with Archimedean, density, nested intervals) → roots →
  powers and logs → length, area, trig → uncountability. Reasons on
  record: √2 closes on the first page of payments regardless
  (simplest use of the axiom), so the geometry can wait and land late
  as the high point, now directly against the ending; the
  verification work in the geometry block is the chapter's hardest
  sup manipulation and belongs after practice, not before;
  roots-and-powers-in-one-stroke right after the axiom makes the
  contrast with Ch 1's one-word-at-a-time patching immediate.
  Alternative on file if Ch 1's ending leans hard on the trap image:
  geometry immediately after the axiom.
- **The exponential thread's Ch 2 identity** (decided 2026-08-20; see
  `exponential-thread.md`): aˣ denotes for every real x — **Oresme's
  1360 speculation, cashed** — and Stifel's table becomes fillable in
  principle. The law of exponents is *proved here as a theorem about
  the construction*, not stated as a wish (D1: the wish waits for the
  Part II reversal). **Required sharpening**: state the uniqueness
  argument as a CLASSIFICATION theorem — the functional equation forces
  aʳ on rationals, monotonicity plus density forces the rest, *and
  these are all the monotone solutions*. Part II then drops exactly one
  word from the hypothesis and the monsters walk in. Without this the
  reversal has no bite, since Ch 2 already exhibits a one-parameter
  family.
- **The sting — this chapter's computational identity** (2026-08-20):
  the entries are defined by suprema, so we know they exist and cannot
  compute one. Ch 1 could compute without naming; Ch 2 names without
  computing. Chapters 3–6 are the reunion.
- **The endpoint of an infinite process, informally** (decided
  2026-08-18): for the monotone processes carried from Ch 1
  (Babylonian, polygon values), this chapter says the sup IS the
  endpoint — "the values crowd up against their sup" — stated
  informally, with the named debt that "crowd up" gets its precise
  meaning in Ch 3. The oscillating side-and-diagonal numbers show on
  the page that sup is NOT the general answer (their sup is 3/2, the
  endpoint is √2): the general notion is deliberately left wanting.
- **The axiom makes the new notations denote** (decided 2026-08-20;
  the unification pass). Ch 1 introduced series, products and continued
  fractions as notations for sequences and could evaluate none of them.
  This chapter cashes two of its specimens, by the same move it makes
  everywhere else:
  - **Zeno's sum exists.** Positive terms, increasing partial sums,
    bounded — the axiom supplies the number they never exceed. Whether
    it is 1 is Ch 3's business.
  - **Every symbol in Viète's formula denotes.** The nested roots exist
    one at a time (algebraic track); the partial products are monotone
    and bounded, so the axiom hands over the value. The formula *means
    something*. Whether it equals 2/π is Ch 4's.
  - **The honest gap, already scheduled**: for mixed signs the sup is
    NOT the value — the side-and-diagonal numbers show it on the page —
    so the general notion is deliberately left wanting for Ch 3.
  This gives products and series the part's own architecture, running
  in parallel with number: Ch 1 nothing denotes → Ch 2 everything
  denotes → Ch 3 the first one evaluated → Ch 4 Viète closes.
- **No decimal section in this chapter** (decided 2026-08-18): a
  decimal is a process — a sequence of approximations — so the whole
  treatment lives in Ch 3, opened there by the same one-line sup
  definition. The ending's naming thought survives via "the nameable
  numbers are countable" (algebraics, computables). If Ch 1 adopts
  the decimals shock, this chapter names the IOU on the page (house
  pattern, as with trig).
- Uncountability proof: nested intervals (April's choice, verified) —
  decimals were never a logical prerequisite for the ending.
- Prelude brief (working assignment): beats 3–4 — the reckoning
  (1872) and the door. Still TBD: the example the chapter is
  remembered by.
